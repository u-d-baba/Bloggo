8314\. That’s how old I am in days at the time of publishing this post. That's a pretty large number to imagine in terms of diary pages I had filled if I had maintained one since my birth. If it had existed, it would've occupied a coordinate in the literary space similar to [Niu Lai](https://letterboxd.com/film/niu-lai/) in the filmmaking space — absolutely human but low-quality work which would be dotted with quad stemmed m's and newspaper cutouts of Audis and BMWs.

This diary is an imaginary artefact, but what I do have with me is a shelf of [memory orbs](https://letterboxd.com/film/inside-out-2015/): mostly yellows, some blues, a few reds, and rarer violets and greens. A haze has settled on most of them now, but some, from the last four years of my life, are still crystal-clear...


###### a day in oct'22

> *Beta, Hyderabad aave toh pan khushi thi jaje.*

My final college counselling results were due that evening. I should've been nervous given that I had blundered in my seat choice filling and the past results of allotment rounds were stuck at IIT Hyderabad. Maybe, the exercise of opening the portal every week for roughly a month to see that my stars hadn't budged had tempered my mind to just accept whatever was going to be served and carry on. This was, I think, because life had thrown a lot of curveballs at me in those couple of months. I had cried straight for days after my entrance exam, more so after the answer keys were released and I discovered that my score was worthless. I found a day of joy after an unexpectedly good result, but the next day found me lying on a hospital cot, down with dengue, a rematch after a brutal bout the year before.

I was outside, in a roadside mela, with my family and some other relatives when the allotment result was to be declared. The fact that the topper had dropped out of his IIT seat had been lingering inside my head since morning. *"Maybe, the stars will finally budge"*. When the watch displayed 8:05 p.m., I was standing away from the huddle of my family who were checking my results on a phone. A few minutes later, my dad shouted out... **"GUWAHATI!!! GUWAHATI!!!"** I was overjoyed. Assam had called me back...


###### a day in oct'22

> *Woh result ke baad Quora pe tune hi post dali thi na?*

My first day of classes had ended; the MA101 class by KV Srikanth was a thriller. That course had some reputation among the seniors of being a tough nut to crack but I found it interesting enough to take it on as a challenge. *"Baby, this is what you came for"* was my thought when the class ended because if this is how all our classes were going to be, then those two years would be worth it. Also, sitting next to me during this was a guy from my hostel who had accompanied me for the institute orientation a day before. He fit the archetype of smart people that I kind of expected to find here: a South Indian who couldn't speak Hindi, had cracked olympiads, had shared classrooms with AIR 1, got nerfed by dengue on exam day, and still ended up with a commendable rank... Meet Tanush.

In those early days, we went to classes together and eventually, this connection branched to two other CS guys from our hostel: Tarun and Udbhav. Udbhav was this extroverted lad who was known to half our batch within a week, and Tarun was one of those who did not bother to put a profile picture on his Instagram. Add to that mix Tarun's roommate, Shreyansh, who shared labs with them. It just happened but, on lazy weekend afternoons, on stressful exam nights or random evenings, the bunch of us found ourselves together in our den: that D lobby room which now features a large hole in its door. We still call ourselves *"Randi Rona"*. The topics have changed, but the spirit is very much alive...

###### a day in dec'22

> *Bhai! La Pulga ko kya ho gya?!*

My scrappy playlist called "FIFA", which was, as expected, headlined by "Waka Waka" by Shakira, was playing as six or seven of us waited for the second half of the 2022 WC Final to begin. A month prior, I had dropped a casual invitation to watch the Argentina opener against Saudi Arabia at my room in the hostel group chat. No one came at the start but as the match result was heading into an upset, a crowd had gathered around my tiny laptop screen. As the tournament proceeded, some became regulars for the screenings, including my roommate-cum-opp Sanyam, who had never watched a football match before the WC, but enjoyed trolling me after the opener disaster.

After the final was forced into penalties, the room was throbbing with a crowd of around twenty people: some seated, some standing on the bed, some standing near the door. Our cheers and shouts synced with the shots and the saves as the tiebreaker unfolded. When the final ball was netted, the crowd turned fanatical. This wasn't the first time the hostel had come together like this. There were the infamous GPLs, the *introductory* sessions with seniors, and the initial weeks spent moving as a pack to every campus event labelled "freshers allowed" — including a screening of [The Ring]() and a play called "Peele Scooter Wala Aadmi".

With time, as the semesters went by, our subjects diverged, friend circles branched out, roommates split, and everyone found their own footing in the campus ecosystem. The pack had spread out but not died — everyone knew that home was where the hostel was. 

We are still a bunch of bakchod manas...
